👉 Learn more about L-systems

In 1891, David Hilbert described a remarkable mathematical object: a continuous curve that fills an entire square.

At its nnn-th finite approximation, the length of the curve is given by:

Ln=2n−2−n.L_n = 2^n - 2^{-n}.Ln​=2n−2−n.

As n→∞n\rightarrow\inftyn→∞, its length diverges, yet every point remains inside a square of area one. In the limit, the curve passes through every point of that square!

Formulated more accurately: the Hausdorff dimension of the Hilbert curve equals exactly 2.
