✨ Helion

❤️ Visualizing the beauty of math & physics

Section titled “❤️ Visualizing the beauty of math & physics”

Helion is a browser-native framework for interactive mathematics and physics. It turns the browser into a virtual laboratory where scientific ideas can be explored through code, numerical experiments, and interactive visualizations.

Helion is built around a simple principle: scientific code should express scientific intent. Mathematical and physical concepts such as fields, transformations, forces, geometries, and numerical solvers should be recognizable in the code, rather than hidden behind layers of generic software abstractions.

The result is a JavaScript environment (fast feedback loops!) for building simulations that are both computationally meaningful and visually engaging.

From scientific model to interactive visualization

Section titled “From scientific model to interactive visualization”

Helion separates a scientific model from the way it is visualized. A model describes the mathematical or physical system; transformations modify its state, and solvers advance it according to the equations or numerical methods being used. Views translate that state into a visual representation.

This separation makes it possible to explore the same model through different views, to change how a system is solved without redesigning its visualization, and to build interactive experiments from reusable components.

📌 Interactive 2D and 3D graphics — observe the behavior of a system as it evolves
📌 Browser-based exploration — run the published demonstrations directly in your browser
📌 Built for curiosity — from individual mathematical objects to complex physical simulations

🧠 Core concepts

Section titled “🧠 Core concepts”

Helion organizes scientific simulations around a small set of complementary ideas. Each has a distinct responsibility.

ConceptResponsibilityExample
Model / stateRepresents the system being studiedA body, particle cloud, field, or wave function
TransformationChanges a model or applies a physical or mathematical operationA force, collision, or Fourier transform
SolverComputes the evolution or solution of a mathematical problemA wave-equation or Schrödinger solver
ViewRepresents a model visuallyA sphere, field visualization, curve, or probability-density view
BindingConnects a model to its view and synchronizes their statebody.alwaysWith(view)
SimulationCoordinates time stepping, interaction, and renderingSimulation.with(...).onStep(...)

🔬 One framework, many scientific domains

Section titled “🔬 One framework, many scientific domains”

Helion is intended to support a broad range of mathematical and physical exploration. The same architectural ideas can be used to study very different systems:

  • Classical mechanics: forces, collisions, trajectories, and interacting particles.
  • Fields and waves: scalar and vector fields, electromagnetic phenomena, wave propagation, and interference.
  • Quantum physics: wave functions, quantum potentials, eigenstates, and probability distributions.
  • Mathematics: complex functions, Fourier analysis, parametric surfaces, differential geometry, fractals, and numerical algorithms.
  • Molecular and statistical physics: molecular interactions, particle systems, and thermodynamic behavior.
  • Nature-inspired systems: procedural geometry, growth, and simulations of natural patterns.
  • Astronomy and relativity: visual models of celestial systems and relativistic phenomena.

The published examples are an important part of Helion: they show how the underlying concepts can be used in practice, from compact mathematical demonstrations to more elaborate simulations.

🎓 Designed for learning and exploration

Section titled “🎓 Designed for learning and exploration”

Helion is particularly suited to situations where seeing a system evolve is as important as understanding its equations.

  • 👩🏻‍🎓 Students exploring mathematics and physics through experiments.
  • 👨🏻‍🏫 Educators building interactive demonstrations and explanations.
  • 🧑‍💻 Developers and learners investigating algorithms and developing physical intuition.
  • 🕵️ Researchers and curious minds prototyping models and exploring numerical behavior.

Rather than treating visualization as a final, separate step, Helion brings the model and its visual representation together throughout the experiment. Parameters can be changed, simulations can be restarted, and different representations can help reveal patterns that are difficult to see in equations alone.
