### About
<div class="header_line"></div>

🧠 Original [idea and code](https://rhettallain.com/2019/11/12/modeling-a-spinning-sprinkler/) by Rhett Allain<br/>
🐍 A [VPython demo](https://www.glowscript.org/#/user/zeger.hendrikse/folder/MyPrograms/program/WaterSprinkler) is available as well, see [water_sprinkler.py](https://github.com/zhendrikse/physics-in-python/blob/main/vpython/water_sprinkler.py)<br/>
👉 The outflow velocity is given by:

$$
\overrightarrow{v} = -\overrightarrow{r} \times \omega + a v_\text{water} \dfrac{\overrightarrow{r}}{\| \overrightarrow{r} \|}
$$

👉  $a=+1 \rightarrow \text{shoot outward}$ and $a=−1 \rightarrow \text{shoot inward}$.

### Model and implementation

<div class="header_line"></div>

The sprinkler consists of a rotating arm with two outlets at opposite ends.
Water droplets are released periodically from these outlets and then move
freely with constant velocity.

The sprinkler rotates about the $z$-axis with angular velocity

$$
\boldsymbol{\omega} =\omega\hat{\mathbf{z}}
$$

If $\theta$ denotes the current rotation angle, the position of an outlet
relative to the center is

$$
\overrightarrow{r}=\frac{L}{2}
\begin{pmatrix}
\cos\theta \\
\sin\theta
\end{pmatrix}
$$

where $L$ is the length of the sprinkler arm.

The velocity of an outlet due to the rotation of the sprinkler is the tangential velocity

$$
\overrightarrow{v}_{\mathrm{tan}}=-\overrightarrow{r}\times\boldsymbol{\omega}
$$

When a droplet leaves the sprinkler, it also receives a radial velocity component.
Its magnitude is controlled by the water velocity parameter:

$$
\overrightarrow{v}_{\mathrm{rad}}=a v_w \hat{\mathbf{r}},
$$

where $v_w$ is the water velocity and

$$
a=\begin{cases}
+1 & \text{for outward flow}\\
-1 & \text{for inward flow}.
\end{cases}
$$

The initial velocity of a released droplet is therefore

$$
\boxed{
\overrightarrow{v}_0= -\overrightarrow{r}\times\boldsymbol{\omega}+a v_w \hat{\mathbf{r}}
}
$$

and the droplet subsequently moves with constant velocity:

$$
\boxed{
\overrightarrow{r}(t)=\overrightarrow{r}_0+\overrightarrow{v}_0t
}
$$

No forces are applied to the droplets after they leave the sprinkler.
In particular, gravity is not included in this model.
The simulation therefore represents the kinematics of the rotating sprinkler
and the emitted water rather than a complete model of a physical water sprinkler.

### Droplet management

The simulation uses a fixed pool of droplets rather than continuously creating new objects.
Each `WaterBeam` owns a pool of reusable droplets. When a droplet leaves the visible region,
it becomes inactive and can be reused for a subsequent emission.

This avoids continuously allocating and discarding objects while the simulation is running.
The views remain bound to the droplets throughout the simulation; an inactive droplet is
simply hidden.

### Time stepping

The physical timestep used to update the sprinkler and the droplets is independent
of the rendering/update interval specified by `Simulation.runsEvery()`.

At every simulation step, the droplets are first advanced using their constant velocities,
new droplets are released according to the emission frequency, and the sprinkler
angle is advanced according to

$$
\theta \leftarrow \theta+\omega \Delta t
$$

The fixed physical timestep allows the same model to evolve consistently on different devices,
while `Simulation.runsEvery()` controls how frequently the simulation is visually updated.
