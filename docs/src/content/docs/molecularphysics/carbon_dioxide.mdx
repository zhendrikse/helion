### Greenhouse effect of carbon dioxide
<div class="header_line"></div>

Why is carbon dioxide so important to global warming?

The global warming / greenhouse effect is mainly due to particular resonance of the carbon dioxide molecules.
In this animation, a carbon dioxide molecule is exposed to an alternating electric field.
Discover which frequencies the molecule responds to and if so, how.

### Model and implementation

<div class="header_line"></div>

The carbon dioxide molecule is modeled as a three-body system consisting of one carbon atom and two oxygen atoms.

The equilibrium configuration is linear, with the carbon atom between the two oxygen atoms.

For the bond potential, we use Hooke's law:

$$
\overrightarrow{F}=-k\left(r-r_0\right)\hat{\mathbf{r}}
$$

where $r$ is the current bond length and $r_0$ is the equilibrium bond length.

For the angular interaction, let

$$
\overrightarrow{v}_1=\overrightarrow{r}_{O_1}-
\overrightarrow{r}_{C}
$$

and

$$
\overrightarrow{v}_2 = \overrightarrow{r}_{O_2}-
\overrightarrow{r}_{C}
$$

Because the equilibrium configuration is linear, the angular displacement is measured as the deviation from a straight line:

$$
\theta = \arccos
\left(
\frac{
\overrightarrow{v}_1\cdot(-\overrightarrow{v}_2)
}{
|\overrightarrow{v}_1| |\overrightarrow{v}_2|
}
\right)
$$

Thus, $\theta=0$ corresponds to a perfectly linear molecule. A bent molecule has $\theta>0$.

The direction of the bending force is determined from the sum of the two bond vectors:

$$
\hat{\mathbf{b}}=\frac{
\overrightarrow{v}_1+\overrightarrow{v}_2
}{
|\overrightarrow{v}_1+\overrightarrow{v}_2|
}
$$

The force applied to each oxygen atom is

$$
\boxed{
\overrightarrow{F}_O = -k_t r_0\theta \hat{\mathbf{b}}
}
$$

while the carbon atom receives the compensating force

$$
\boxed{\overrightarrow{F}_C =-2\overrightarrow{F}_O}
$$

The angular interaction is therefore a **three-body force**: the force on each atom depends on the positions of both bonds simultaneously. In the JavaScript implementation, the bending force is consequently applied to both bonds together:

```js
co2.bond1.and(co2.bond2).apply(bendForce);
```

This implementation follows the force formulation used in the original VPython CO₂ example. In particular, the angular displacement is defined as the deviation from linearity rather than as the difference from an equilibrium angle of $\pi$.

Finally, the interaction with the external electric field is modeled using the electric force

$$
\boxed{\overrightarrow{F} = q\overrightarrow{E}}
$$

where $q$ is the charge of the atom and $\overrightarrow{E}$ is the local electric field.

At each simulation step, the Coulomb, bond-stretching, and angular forces are first accumulated on all three atoms. Only after all forces have been applied are the atoms integrated forward in time. This preserves the same force/update structure as the original VPython model while using the JavaScript force framework.
