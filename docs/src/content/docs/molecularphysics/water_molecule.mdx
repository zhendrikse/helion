### Oscillating water molecule
<div class="header_line"></div>

Why is carbon dioxide so important to global warming?

Why does water vapor absorb in so many wavelengths? Due to its non-linear shape, it oscillates in many ways!
See below how frantically a water molecule may respond to an alternating electric field.

### Model and implementation
<div class="header_line"></div>

For the bond potential, we have used Hooke's law:

$$
F = -c (u - u_0)
$$

For the angle potential, we have:
1. The angle is determined from **both bonds at the same time**.
2. The force on both H-atoms is exact:
$$
\overrightarrow{F}_H = k_t r_0 \left(\theta - \theta_0\right)\dfrac{\overrightarrow{v}_1 + \overrightarrow{v}_2}{\|\overrightarrow{v}_1 + \overrightarrow{v}_2\|}
$$
3. The oxygen atom gets the exact opposite sum $\overrightarrow{F}_O = -2\overrightarrow{F}_H$	​
4. The `TorqueForce` has therefore to be apply to `water.bond1.and(water.bond2)`, not separately on `bond1` and `bond2`.

Finally, for the electric force, we have used the Coulomb force:

$$
\overrightarrow{F} = q\overrightarrow{E}
$$
