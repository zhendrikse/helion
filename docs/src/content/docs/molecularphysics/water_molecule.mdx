### Oscillating water molecule
<div class="header_line"></div>

Why is carbon dioxide so important to global warming?

Why does water vapor absorb in so many wavelengths? Due to its non-linear shape, it oscillates in many ways!
See below how frantically a water molecule may respond to an alternating electric field.

### Model and implementation
<div class="header_line"></div>

The water molecule is modeled as a three-body system consisting of one oxygen atom and two hydrogen atoms.

For the bond potential, we use Hooke's law:

$$
\overrightarrow{F}=-k\left(r-r_0\right)\hat{\mathbf{r}}
$$

where $r$ is the current bond length and $r_0$ is the equilibrium bond length.

For the angular interaction, the angle is determined from **both bonds simultaneously**. Let

$$
\overrightarrow{v}_1= \overrightarrow{r}_{H_1}-
\overrightarrow{r}_{O}
$$

and

$$
\overrightarrow{v}_2= \overrightarrow{r}_{H_2}-
\overrightarrow{r}_{O}
$$

The molecular angle is

$$
\theta = \arccos
\left(
\frac{
\overrightarrow{v}_1\cdot\overrightarrow{v}_2
}{
|\overrightarrow{v}_1| |\overrightarrow{v}_2|
}
\right)
$$

The angular force is directed along the normalized bisector of the two bonds:

$$
\hat{\mathbf{b}} =\frac{
\overrightarrow{v}_1+\overrightarrow{v}_2
}{
|\overrightarrow{v}_1+\overrightarrow{v}_2|
}
$$

The force applied to each hydrogen atom is

$$
\boxed{
\overrightarrow{F}_H=k_t r_0
\left(\theta-\theta_0\right)
\hat{\mathbf{b}}
}
$$

where $\theta_0$ is the equilibrium bond angle.

Since the two hydrogen atoms receive the same angular force, the corresponding force on the oxygen atom is

$$
\boxed{
\overrightarrow{F}_O=-2\overrightarrow{F}_H
}
$$

This ensures that the total angular force on the molecule is zero.

The angular interaction is therefore a **three-body force**: it depends on both bonds at the same time. Consequently, the `TorqueForce` must be applied to the pair

```js
water.bond1.and(water.bond2)
```

rather than independently to `bond1` and `bond2`.

Finally, the interaction with the external electric field is modeled using the electric force

$$
\boxed{
\overrightarrow{F}=q\overrightarrow{E}
}
$$

where $q$ is the charge of the atom and $\overrightarrow{E}$ is the local electric field.

At each simulation step, all forces are first accumulated on the three atoms. The atoms are then integrated only after all forces have been applied. This mirrors the force/update structure of the original VPython model while allowing the JavaScript implementation to handle the interactions through its force framework.
