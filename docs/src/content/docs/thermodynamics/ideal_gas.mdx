### Maxwell-Boltzmann 2D vis-à-vis 3D
<div class="header_line"><br/></div>

#### Maxwell-Boltzmann velocity distributions for $\overrightarrow{v}$

The velocity distribution for $f(\overrightarrow{v}) , d^n\overrightarrow{v}$ is given by

##### 3D case

$$
f(\overrightarrow{v}) d^3\overrightarrow{v} = \left(\frac{m}{2 \pi k_B T}\right)^{3/2} \exp\Big(-\frac{m v^2}{2 k_B T}\Big) d^3\overrightarrow{v}
$$

##### 2D case

$$
f(\overrightarrow{v}) d^2\overrightarrow{v} = \left(\frac{m}{2 \pi k_B T}\right) \exp\Big(-\frac{m v^2}{2 k_B T}\Big) d^2\overrightarrow{v}
$$

* We have $v^2 = v_x^2 + v_y^2 + v_z^2$ in 3 dimensions and $v^2 = v_x^2 + v_y^2$ in 2 dimensions.
* Normalisation because the integral volume $d^n\overrightarrow{v}$ depends on the dimension.

#### Maxwell-Boltzmann velocity distributions for $\|\overrightarrow{v}\|$

In the graphs we reduce to a **radial distribution**, so we take
the “surface of the cirkel/sphere”:

$$
d^n\overrightarrow{v} = v^{n-1} dv , d\Omega_n
$$

where $d\Omega_n$ denotes the angular part.
This amounts to an additional factor $v$ for 2D, $v^2$ for 3D.

##### 3D case

$$
f(v) dv = 4\pi \left(\frac{m}{2 \pi k_B T}\right)^{3/2} v^2 \exp\Big(-\frac{m v^2}{2 k_B T}\Big) dv
$$

##### 2D case

$$
f(v) dv = \frac{m}{k_B T} , v , \exp\Big(-\frac{m v^2}{2 k_B T}\Big) dv
$$

* **Factor $v^{n-1}$** originates from the velocities with the same speeds (surface of a cirkel/sphere).
* This is what is used in the code, e.g. `value = (v / T) * exp(-v*v / (2 * T))` in 2D.
