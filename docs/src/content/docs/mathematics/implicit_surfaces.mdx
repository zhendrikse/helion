#### Gyroid lattice

From [WikiPedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyroid) we learn that

> A gyroid is an infinitely connected [triply periodic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triply_periodic_minimal_surface)
> [minimal surface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minimal_surface) discovered by
> [Alan Schoen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Schoen) in 1970 [1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyroid#cite_note-1)
> [2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyroid#cite_note-hoffman-2).
> It arises naturally in polymer science and biology, as an interface with high surface area.

- **Triple periodic**: The pattern repeats infinitely in three directions in space.
- **Mechanically isotropic**: It distributes stresses evenly in all directions rather than via rigid beams.
- **No straight lines or mirror surfaces**: The surface consists entirely of smooth, continuous curves.
- **Self-supporting**: Due to its unique geometry, it is ideal for additive manufacturing (3D printing) without the need for support material everywhere.
