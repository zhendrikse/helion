In 1891, [David Hilbert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Hilbert)
described a remarkable mathematical object: a continuous curve
that fills an entire square.

At its $n$-th finite approximation, the length of the curve
is given by:

$$
L_n = 2^n - 2^{-n}.
$$

As $n\rightarrow\infty$, its length diverges, yet every point remains inside a square
of area one. In the limit, the curve passes through every point of that square!

Formulated more accurately: the [Hausdorff dimension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hausdorff_dimension)
of the [Hilbert curve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hilbert_curve) equals exactly 2.
