> The **Doppler effect** is the change in frequency of a wave in relation to an observer who is moving relative to the wave source.

### Doppler effect demo
<div class="header_line"></div>

Move the observer by dragging the person horizontally. The expanding circles show the sound wavefronts emitted by the moving source. Change the source frequency, source velocity, or wave propagation speed to explore the effect.

### The observed frequency
<div class="header_line"></div>

For a stationary observer and a source moving with velocity component $v_s\cos(\theta)$ along the line from source to observer, the observed frequency is

$$
\boxed{
f_{obs}=f_{src}\frac{c}{c-v_s\cos(\theta)}
}
$$

where $c$ is the wave propagation speed and $\theta$ is the angle between the source velocity and the direction towards the observer. The sign of the radial velocity determines whether the source approaches or recedes from the observer.

The simulation measures the time interval between successive wavefront arrivals,

$$
f_{obs}=\frac{1}{\Delta t},
$$

and plots that measured value together with the value predicted by the Doppler formula. Moving the observer changes the direction of observation and therefore the radial component of the source velocity.
